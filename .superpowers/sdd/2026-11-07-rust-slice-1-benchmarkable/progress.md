Ruling (snapshot publication design; replaces the CAS-retry protocol of 42a9019e): order by serialization, not by comparing rows. One writer-side mutex W covers "read the database, then publish" for the two authoritative paths — full rebuild, and credential patch (the targeted read-by-id moves INSIDE the section). Each read starts a fresh transaction inside W, so whichever section runs later has seen everything the earlier one published; the later one simply wins: a patch replaces its row with what it read (removes it if the row is gone, never inserts), a rebuild replaces the snapshot. No version/enabled/timestamp comparison, no retry loop, rebuild does exactly one read. Health patches do not take W and keep their existing rcu semantics against rebuild (as accepted in slice 1). Request-path loads never touch W. Cost: a credential patch may wait for one in-flight rebuild read (ms) — refresh is rare and already includes a network round trip. Cost if wrong: time; the regressions (R1 gated health-patch traffic during a rebuild, R2 reverse order, the three earlier interleavings, delete-vs-patch) are the bar, and the implementer may propose another protocol only if it passes all of them with bounded work.
